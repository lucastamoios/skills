# Decision guidance from the foundations

These are concise applications of the primary sources in [sources.md](sources.md).
They are not book summaries or a mandatory process. The routing rules are an original
synthesis for this suite. Read only the section relevant to the current question.

## Manager: choose a decision, not a phase

Ries frames progress as learning about the business through evidence. Torres connects
an intended outcome to customer opportunities, possible solutions, and assumption tests.
Use those distinctions to identify where a conversation needs more evidence. Return
to an earlier assumption when the result calls it into question. Do not create an
opportunity tree from imagined customer needs or require a complete tree to begin.

Useful questions are: what decision are we making, what must be true for it to work,
what evidence do we already have, and what could change our mind? The manager should
answer what it can from context and ask only what remains important.

## Explore: understand a real situation

Blank distinguishes testing customer hypotheses from collecting feature requests.
Fitzpatrick's work addresses biased feedback in customer conversations. Use neutral
questions about a specific event, current workarounds, consequences, and commitments.
The detailed prompts below are our application, not quotations from either author:

- Ask what happened the last time the problem arose.
- Ask how the person handled it and what that required.
- Ask who else was involved and how a purchase or process change would be approved.
- Ask for an example or artifact when it would resolve a meaningful uncertainty.

Separate the customer's problem from the founder's proposed solution. Assess access
to customers and the effect on existing projects using the user's actual circumstances.
Public research can identify possibilities; it cannot replace observation of the
particular customers whose behavior the business depends on.

## Offer: connect differences to customer value

Dunford starts from the alternatives customers actually consider, then connects
differences to value, customers who care, and market category. Strategyzer's Value
Proposition Canvas distinguishes customer jobs, pains, and gains from the products
and services intended to address them. Use these as lenses when they clarify an offer;
do not require a canvas or declare fit because its boxes are filled.

In this suite, the output is a provisional promise and buying arrangement that can
be tested. Treat a new segment or category as a hypothesis. Explain a meaningful
tradeoff instead of claiming universal superiority. Researching competitors informs
the offer but does not demonstrate that the user's customer will buy it.

For public-sector work, Blank and Osterwalder's Mission Model Canvas adds a useful
lens on beneficiaries, adoption, stakeholder support, budgets, and mission outcomes.
Apply that lens to the public organization while separately examining the supplier's
revenue, costs, delivery obligations, and repeatability. The canvas does not establish
procurement rules, funding, contract status, or municipal approval.

## Experiment: make the learning decision explicit

Ries's learning cycle supports planning from the intended learning back to the test.
Strategyzer's Test Card makes the hypothesis, experiment, measure, and success
criterion explicit. Translate these into a credible observation with bounded effort.
Set the criterion before collecting results and identify what a negative or inconclusive
result would mean. The minimum useful test depends on the claim, not on a universal
interview count, conversion rate, budget, or product feature list.

Our adaptation adds explicit decision branches and an inconclusive outcome. It also
checks customer authority, procurement timing, recruitment bias, delivery economics,
and execution permissions when those factors matter. These are contextual design
choices, not named authors' universal thresholds.

## Review: separate observation from interpretation

Strategyzer's Learning Card separates the tested belief, observation, learning, and
resulting action. Use that distinction to compare actual evidence with the original
expectation. Ries's pivot or persevere question concerns a change in a fundamental
hypothesis; it does not require abandoning everything learned.

Our review also allows a narrower conclusion, an inconclusive result, a pause, or a
targeted follow-up. Reopen the assumption contradicted by evidence. When data support
a decision, do not keep researching merely to complete a framework.
