# Business Experiment

Read [the shared contract](../business/references/contract.md) unless already active.
Use the experiment section of [the foundations](../business/references/foundations.md).

## Start with the decision

Identify what decision the test informs and the assumption that could change it.
Use the consequence of being wrong, remaining uncertainty, and cost of learning to
select the next test. Test an existential dependency before optimizing a minor feature.
Do not use an arbitrary score to turn uncertainty into permission to build.

Plan backward: decide what needs to be learned, what observation could support or
contradict it, then choose the smallest credible way to obtain that observation.
Do not automatically prescribe software, a landing page, A/B testing, or a paid pilot.

Choose a method that matches the claim. Interviews about recent behavior can reveal
process and constraints. An offer test can reveal willingness to commit. A concierge
service can reveal value and delivery work. A technical spike can reveal feasibility.
A cohort observation can reveal continued use. None proves all the others. In a
concierge test the customer may know the work is manual; do not require deception.

## Define a compact experiment

Agree enough detail to execute and interpret the test:

- State the hypothesis with the relevant segment and situation.
- Describe the observation, method, participants, and recruitment source.
- Specify the success criterion, failure criterion, and inconclusive outcome before
  results arrive. Use a justified range or qualitative criterion when small samples
  make percentages misleading. Label agent-proposed thresholds as proposals.
- Define the time and effort bound, cost assumptions, and material operational limits.
- State what action each result would support and what the test cannot establish.
- Identify the owner and execution authorization where they are needed.

For small B2B or public-sector samples, examine individual cases, authority to buy,
selection bias, and procurement timing. A deadline with no sale is not automatically
proof of no demand. Do not invent statistical significance or apply high-volume
consumer conversion benchmarks to a handful of interviews.

Distinguish the cheapest test from a test that can actually answer the question.
For a working product, use existing evidence before imposing another experiment;
delegate retrieving that evidence. Simulated tests can rehearse execution but cannot
be recorded as customer validation.

## Hand off without changing the hypothesis

Return the proposed test and decision branches to the manager. Preserve the original
criterion when the test starts. If the scope or criterion changes, record why and
when rather than retrospectively declaring success. Once results exist, use
business-review. Drafting a test does not launch it or authorize external actions.
