# Business Offer

Read [the shared contract](../business/references/contract.md) unless already active.
Use the offer section of [the foundations](../business/references/foundations.md).

## Start from the customer and alternatives

Reuse available discovery. Establish whose situation the offer addresses and what
that person does today, including manual work, another service, or doing nothing.
Distinguish the user, buyer, approver, and partner when they differ. If that distinction
is unknown and changes the offer, resolve it before pretending to know the price.
Use a provisional segment when necessary and label it as a hypothesis.

Work from alternatives to meaningful differences, the customer value those differences
enable, the customers who care most, and the market context that makes the value clear.
Do not define differentiation as merely being better, easier, or powered by AI.
Delegate research on competitors, market categories, and pricing to subagents. A
competitor benchmark suggests an anchor; it does not establish willingness to pay.

## Formulate a testable promise

Express an offer in ordinary language: for whom, in what situation, what outcome,
what is delivered, what the customer must do, and what commitment is requested.
Include delivery limits and assumptions that materially affect the promise. Avoid
guarantees unsupported by capability or evidence.

Discuss channel and buying process as well as the product. Determine whether the
next customer can be reached and who must approve a pilot or purchase. For municipal
work, check procurement constraints separately from beneficiary interest. Public
value and the supplier's financial viability are different questions.

Treat price, packaging, and charging unit as testable hypotheses. Consider customer
value and alternatives alongside acquisition, onboarding, delivery, support, and
ongoing costs. Identify the unknowns instead of inventing precise margins or LTV.
Distinguish one-off service revenue from evidence of repeatable product demand.

## Choose what to test

Produce one concise offer, or a few genuinely distinct alternatives if the choice
is still open. State its evidence, biggest assumption, and the commitment that would
help assess it. Use business-experiment for a real offer test. Return to business-explore
only when the problem or customer uncertainty actually blocks the current choice.
An offer can be a discovery instrument; it need not await complete certainty.

Drafting outreach or a landing page does not authorize sending or publishing it.
Return the offer and recommended next step to the manager. Follow the shared
contract for authorized notes and source attribution.
