# Business Review

Read [the shared contract](../business/references/contract.md) unless already active.
Use the review section of [the foundations](../business/references/foundations.md).

## Reconstruct the claim and evidence

Start with the decision, original hypothesis, and intended criterion. Delegate
retrieval of prior notes, experiment records, and additional evidence. If no criterion
was set in advance, say so; describe the result as exploratory rather than inventing
a retrospective pass threshold.

Separate what happened, what it might mean, and what remains unknown. Inspect the
participants, recruitment, dates, behavior, requested commitment, and observation
window. Include negative outcomes and departures from the planned test. Do not
collapse customer statements, usage, payments, and operating margins into one score.

Compare like with like when possible. Examine cohorts or individual accounts so
that new signups do not hide abandonment. Distinguish acquisition, activation, return
use, payment, renewal, and delivery effort. Do not infer causation from a before/after
change without considering other explanations or prescribe an A/B test without
enough traffic and a meaningful decision.

## Recommend a decision proportionate to the evidence

Consider whether the evidence supports the specific claim, contradicts it, is mixed,
or cannot answer it. Check whether the test reached the intended people and asked
for the relevant behavior. An invalid or underpowered test may be inconclusive;
repeated decisive negative evidence should not be excused indefinitely as bad testing.

Recommend the smallest justified move: continue within the observed scope, change
the offer or channel, investigate an ambiguity, revisit the customer/problem, pause,
or stop the hypothesis. Explain the evidence, tradeoff, and what would reopen the
decision. Preserve learning when pivoting; do not throw away every assumption because
one failed. A single positive pilot does not establish a repeatable business.

Keep business models explicit. A service contract may be worthwhile without proving
product scalability. A public-sector pilot may create value without having cleared
procurement. Evaluate each against its real objective and constraints.

## Continue the same conversation

Give the user a concise recommendation and reason. When he accepts a decision, record
it under the existing authorization with the evidence, limitations, and next action.
Before acceptance, keep the recommendation labeled as a proposal. Route to
business-offer, business-explore, or business-experiment only as the unresolved question
requires. Do not automatically start a new research cycle after a sufficient answer.
