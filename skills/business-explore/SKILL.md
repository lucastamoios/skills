# Business Explore

Read [the shared contract](../business/references/contract.md) unless already active.
Use the exploration section of [the foundations](../business/references/foundations.md).
Keep this a conversation. Reuse what the manager or the user already supplied.

## Understand the situation

Begin with the idea or concern in the user's words. Ask about a concrete customer
situation, the desired outcome, and why the idea matters now. If the customer is
unknown, distinguish plausible segments before deciding which one to investigate.
Do not demand a precise problem statement before allowing free exploration.

Look for recent examples: what triggered the problem, what the person did, how
often it occurs, what it costs them, and what they use instead. Treat the account
as reported evidence until the relevant observation or source is available. Identify
who experiences the problem and who could authorize spending to address it.

When choices become concrete, examine only the dimensions that could change the
decision: problem importance, reachable customers, existing alternatives, delivery
constraints, willingness to commit, founder resources, and opportunity cost. Use
the user's actual portfolio and partner access; delegate missing context to a researcher.
Do not assume a social audience converts into buyers or that a competitor's sales
validate this offer.

## Develop and challenge options

Offer a small set of materially different directions when alternatives help. Explain
the tradeoff behind each. Explore freely while the question is open; avoid forcing
a predetermined number of ideas or producing a scorecard after every comment.

For a promising direction, name what must be true and the consequence if it is false.
Prioritize uncertainties by consequence and missing evidence, then consider how cheaply
they can be investigated. Do not manufacture confidence scores or average away a fatal
constraint. Ask for missing personal preferences instead of inferring them.

Distinguish a potential standalone product, a service, and an improvement to an existing
project when that choice matters. An existing commitment changes the question to
what to do next; it is not a reason to refuse exploration or restart from zero.

## Leave a useful next step

Summarize the promising direction, its strongest evidence, the decisive unknown,
and the recommended next action. Explain why that action helps. If the segment and
problem are plausible enough to articulate a test offer, suggest business-offer.
If the key uncertainty is testable already, use business-experiment directly.
If new evidence arrived during the conversation, use business-review as needed.
Return these findings to the manager without repeating intake.

This skill adapts the portfolio and distribution lens of Business Brainstorm and
the conversational approach of the existing brainstorm skill. It uses customer
discovery and interview principles; see [sources](../business/references/sources.md).
